Mission

Help teams run critical functions and AI on shared platforms, with behaviour they can predict, defend and evidence.

In the Physical AI era, cars, robots, factories and edge systems run real-time, safety- and security-critical workloads on the same silicon as non-deterministic noisy neighbours such as AI inference.

Safety and security both come down to three questions: Is the system intact? Is it behaving as intended? Can it recover to a safe, secure state when it isn’t?

My focus is designing the answers in, verifying them through system-level analysis, measurement and attestation, and building in resilience: fail-safe or fail-operational at runtime, secure recovery and incident response in the field.

An open, platform-neutral specification for attributing interference between AI inference and real-time control on shared silicon: an interference taxonomy, an attribution protocol and a results schema with mandatory provenance. About the mark

Non-deterministic in, predictable out

Safety engineering never promises zero failure. Security engineering never promises zero vulnerabilities. Now AI is acting in the physical world. So what can engineers actually promise?

The orange dots are the non-deterministic world: faults, adversaries, the unknown, and now AI inference. Some converge through the sky-blue point, where engineering happens: isolation, diverse redundancy, independence and measurement. They resolve into navy: predictable behaviour, within known limits. The navy L is the deterministic frame that holds it all, and the axes it is measured against.

Some dots never converge. That is residual risk, acknowledged and managed with evidence, not wished away. So we can’t promise that nothing will go wrong. We can promise that we know where it can go wrong, that we’ve contained it, and that our evidence doesn’t come from the system being judged.
